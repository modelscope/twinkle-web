---
title: Supervised Fine-Tuning
description: What the SFT loss trains, and how to read the curve honestly.
---

SFT maximizes the likelihood of the demonstration tokens. Two consequences
shape everything else:

1. **Only masked-in tokens carry loss.** In chat-format data the template masks
   the prompt; the model is trained on responses, not on your instructions.
   Pre-training (`swift pt`) removes the mask — every token trains.
2. **The curve reflects headroom, not effort.** An already-instruct model fine-
   tuned on general assistant data has little left to learn: loss falls from
   ~1.8 to ~1.6 and plateaus. That is a correct run, not a broken one. On new
   knowledge (e.g. self-cognition) the same setup falls 2.6 → 0.3.

Reading a run:

- loss down + grad_norm finite and shrinking → healthy;
- loss flat on general data → expected; switch to a dataset with real headroom
  to verify the pipeline;
- loss NaN in bf16 → check the attention kernel first (pin
  `--attn_impl flash_attention_2`);
- eval loss diverging from train loss → overfitting; cut epochs or add data.

LoRA vs full-parameter is a capacity/cost dial, not a different algorithm: the
loss, the data contract and the curve semantics are identical.
