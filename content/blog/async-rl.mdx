---
title: Async RL Design
description: Decoupling sampling from training — staleness is the design variable.
---

Synchronous RL alternates: sample a batch, train on it, sync weights, repeat.
The GPUs doing sampling idle during training and vice versa. Async RL removes
the barrier — samplers keep generating while trainers keep updating — and pays
for it with **staleness**: a rollout was produced by an older policy than the
one being updated.

The design therefore revolves around three controls:

1. **Weight publishing cadence.** How often trained weights reach the samplers.
   More frequent sync = fresher rollouts, more sync traffic.
2. **Staleness bound.** How old a rollout may be when consumed. Trajectories
   carry the policy version that produced them; the trainer can reject or
   down-weight rollouts beyond the bound instead of silently training on
   off-policy data.
3. **Placement.** Disaggregated groups (samplers on their own GPUs, trainers on
   theirs) make the pipeline physically continuous; colocate cannot be fully
   async because the phases share the same cards.

What stays invariant: the reward contract, the advantage computation and the
checkpoint semantics are identical to synchronous RL — asynchrony changes
*when* data arrives, not *what* a training step means. That is why the same
recipe can run in both modes by configuration.
