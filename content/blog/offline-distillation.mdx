---
title: Offline Distillation
description: Knowledge transfer through data — and when to go on-policy.
---

Offline distillation is SFT on a teacher's outputs. The mechanism is worth
stating plainly: the student never sees the teacher's logits or weights; it
fits the *behavior* the teacher produced. That makes it cheap (no teacher in
the training loop) and robust (no distribution mismatch machinery), at the
price of an upper bound — the student learns what the teacher wrote, not
everything the teacher knows.

Pipeline:

1. Fix a prompt set covering the target capability.
2. Generate teacher responses (`swift infer` against the teacher, or any
   serving endpoint); keep them in messages format.
3. SFT the student on that jsonl. Filter aggressively: wrong teacher answers
   become wrong student behavior.

Go **on-policy** (GKD/OPSD in SWIFT) when the student's own mistakes matter:
the student samples, the teacher scores or provides logits on *those* samples,
and training corrects the student's actual distribution. That requires
rollouts, so it lives in the RL infrastructure — more expensive, tighter
transfer.

Decision rule: fixed prompts + much stronger teacher → offline; the student
must improve on its own failure modes → on-policy.
