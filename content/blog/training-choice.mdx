---
title: Choosing a Training Method
description: SFT, preference alignment, distillation or RL — decision criteria.
---

Four families, one question: **where does the training signal come from?**

| Method | Signal | Cost | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| SFT | labeled demonstrations | lowest | you have good input→output pairs |
| Preference alignment (DPO/KTO/…) | chosen vs rejected pairs | low–medium | you can rank outputs but not write ideals |
| Distillation | a stronger teacher | medium | a better model exists and can generate |
| Online RL (GRPO/PPO/…) | a reward function on fresh samples | highest | correctness is checkable (math, code, tools) |

Rules of thumb:

1. **Start with SFT.** It is the cheapest signal and the baseline every other
   method is measured against. If SFT on your data does not move the metric,
   fix the data before reaching for RL.
2. **Preference alignment when ranking is easier than writing.** DPO-family
   methods need no sampler and no reward model; they are supervised-style runs.
3. **RL only with a trustworthy reward.** A policy optimizes exactly what the
   reward measures — reward hacking is the default failure mode, not the
   exception. Verifiable rewards (exact-match math, unit tests) are the safe
   starting point.
4. **Distillation transfers capability, RL discovers it.** If the teacher can
   already do the task, distillation is usually cheaper than RL.

In SWIFT all four share one configuration surface — switching methods changes
`--task_type` / `--rlhf_type` and the data, not the infrastructure.
