---
title: Online RL
description: Rollouts, rewards, and why GRPO needs no critic.
---

Online RL closes a loop that SFT leaves open: the model generates, the world
scores, the model updates. Three pieces carry the whole design:

**Rollout.** A fast inference engine (vLLM) samples completions from the
*current* policy. Training weights are synced into the engine before every
rollout round — otherwise the sampler drifts from the policy being trained.
Placement is either colocate (sampler and trainer share GPUs, sleeping between
phases) or disaggregate (each owns a GPU group).

**Reward.** A function from completion to scalar: exact-match accuracy, unit
tests, a reward model, or a plugin. The policy will optimize exactly this
function, so its failure modes become the policy's failure modes.

**Advantage.** GRPO's trick: sample a *group* of completions per prompt and use
the group statistics as the baseline — `(r - mean(group)) / std(group)`. A
critic network would learn that baseline; the group gives it for free. The
update is then a PPO-style clipped surrogate on that advantage, optionally with
a KL penalty to a frozen reference.

Reading a run: watch **reward**, not loss. The policy-gradient loss oscillates
around zero by construction; `train/total_reward` climbing is the signal.
Flat reward at zero usually means the task is too hard for the current policy,
the completion is truncated before the answer, or the scorer cannot extract the
answer — check those three before concluding "no learning".
